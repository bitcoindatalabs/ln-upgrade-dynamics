\section{Did Disclosure Move Operators?}
\label{sec:exposure}

\subsection{Advisories gossip can see}
We collected every public security advisory for LND, CLN, Eclair and LDK from January~2022 to the cutoff (release
notes, Lightning Labs' advisory index, Delving Bitcoin and authors' write-ups), recording the affected versions, the
first fixed release and the disclosure date, each from the source cited in Table~\ref{tab:advisories}. An advisory is
visible in gossip when its fix boundary coincides with the start of a release line (LINE), or falls inside a line but
leaves whole lines affected or fixed on either side (PARTIAL, which gives bounds). Fixes inside the newest line, such
as CLN 26.06.7--26.06.9 and Eclair 0.14.1--0.14.3, are patch releases and invisible. Eclair and LDK have too few live
public nodes with a known line (about ten Eclair nodes) for shares to be meaningful, so Table~\ref{tab:advisories}
lists the \NAdvRows{} LND and CLN entries, covering \NAdvTotal{} advisories.

Two clocks run for each advisory. Fixes usually ship long before the vulnerability is disclosed: the median gap
between the first fixed release and disclosure is \FixToDiscMedian{} days, and the six advisories Lightning Labs
published on 11~August~2026 concerned bugs fixed in LND 0.19.0, released \FixToDiscLLBatch{} days earlier. A node
still on an affected line at disclosure has therefore usually skipped a release that was already available for many
months. Exposure is the share of live nodes (excluding the managed fleet) on an affected line, with the share of
their live capacity alongside. It is largest where the fix is youngest: when Teinturier disclosed that LND accepted
too few confirmations on cooperative closes, \FixToDiscLndCloseconf{} days after the fix shipped in 0.21.0,
\ExpLndCloseconfDisc\% of live LND nodes, holding \ExpCapLndCloseconfDisc\% of live LND capacity, were on an
affected line.

\subsection{Response to disclosure}
\label{sec:response}
If operators act on advisories, nodes should leave the affected lines faster after a disclosure than before it. For
every advisory with whole lines affected and enough snapshots on both sides (at least six of the eight weeks before
and three of the four weeks after), we take, at the start of each week, the live nodes on a wholly affected line,
and count those first seen on a later line within the week. The rate is moves per node-week, and we compare the
weeks before and after disclosure by their rate ratio. Moves are not independent: a platform push or a group of
operators moves many nodes in the same week, and the weekly counts vary up to \RespMaxDisp{} times more than Poisson
counts would. The 95\% confidence intervals are therefore quasi-Poisson, scaled by that dispersion, and we add an exact
permutation test of whether the weekly rates after disclosure exceed those before. Platform pushes could produce a
jump that no operator chose, so we also report the ratio without moves signed within 48~hours after a platform
release that ships the new line (\S\ref{sec:waves}). The comparison has a known bias: the rate of leaving a line
falls as the line ages and its most attentive operators leave, so the before-window has the higher baseline, most of
all when a disclosure follows a release by only a few months. A ratio near one therefore rules out only a large
response.

Twelve disclosures qualify, nine for LND and three for CLN (Table~\ref{tab:response},
Fig.~\ref{fig:response}). Only CLN's two disclosures of 19~July~2026, memory-exhaustion vulnerabilities in gossipd
(fixed in 26.04) and connectd (fixed in 25.09)~\cite{pratap2026clndos}, show a clear increase. Among live CLN nodes on
lines before 26.04, the weekly rate rose from \WkBeforeAllClnGossipd\% to \WkAfterAllClnGossipd\% (ratio
\RRAllClnGossipd, 95\% CI \RRLoAllClnGossipd--\RRHiAllClnGossipd; outside platform releases permutation
$p=\PPermOpClnGossipd$, the only one of the twelve tests below a Bonferroni threshold of $0.05/12$), and for lines
before 25.09 from
\WkBeforeAllClnConnectd\% to \WkAfterAllClnConnectd\% (\RRAllClnConnectd, \RRLoAllClnConnectd--\RRHiAllClnConnectd).
The two groups responded differently. The two fix boundaries define them; we compare the nodes one line behind
with the older lines because, once we dated BTCPay's release, it explained most moves from the older lines, so this
comparison was made after seeing the data. The \AtRiskClnRecent{} nodes one line behind, on 25.09--25.12, moved steadily
over the four weeks (indented row of Table~\ref{tab:response}): without the moves that followed platform releases their
ratio is \RROpClnRecent{} (\RRLoOpClnRecent--\RRHiOpClnRecent), and each of the four weeks after disclosure had a
higher rate than any of the seven before (permutation $p=\PPermOpClnRecent$, the smallest value the test can give
with eleven weeks). The \AtRiskClnConnectd{} nodes on older lines moved mostly in the
48~hours after BTCPay's security release of 7~August, which installed CLN 26.06.1 on every BTCPay deployment that
updated; without those moves their ratio is \RROpClnConnectd{} (\RRLoOpClnConnectd--\RRHiOpClnConnectd;
$p=\PPermOpClnConnectd$). The operators who acted on CLN's disclosure were those already close to current; the ones
further behind moved when their platform's own advisory had them update. A month later, the ping-flood
vulnerability, fixed at the same 25.09 boundary, was disclosed~\cite{cestari2026pingflood}, and the next day the
maintainers asked operators to upgrade or restart offline~\cite{coreln2026offline}. The \AtRiskClnPing{} nodes still
on older lines may have moved somewhat faster, but not clearly: \RRAllClnPing{} (\RRLoAllClnPing--\RRHiAllClnPing),
and outside platform releases, whose removal takes BTCPay's push out of the before-window, \RROpClnPing{}
(\RRLoOpClnPing--\RRHiOpClnPing; $p=\PPermOpClnPing$). Part of the response to that call does not appear in these
rates: a node that restarted offline as asked left the live set and counts as not having moved, so leaving, which
\S\ref{sec:fates} measures, competes with upgrading.

No LND disclosure produced a detectable increase. Across the nine, the ratios are at most \RespLndMaxRR{}, and
none is significant (permutation $p\geq\RespLndMinP$, with or without platform moves). The intervals are wide,
however. For Lightning Labs' batch of 11~August~2026, the weekly rate was \WkBeforeAllLLBatch\% before and
\WkAfterAllLLBatch\% after (\RRAllLLBatch, \RRLoAllLLBatch--\RRHiAllLLBatch); its before-window holds BTCPay's
security release four days earlier, and without the moves that followed it the ratio is \RROpLLBatch{}
(\RRLoOpLLBatch--\RRHiOpLLBatch). Outside platform releases, a modest increase is compatible with these data, but one
as large as CLN's (\RROpClnRecent) is not. What the rates leave open, the exposed cohort settles
(\S\ref{sec:fates}): two months after the batch, only \PctCohortUpLLBatch\% of the nodes exposed at disclosure had
upgraded. The lowest ratios belong to the cooperative-close disclosure, \FixToDiscLndCloseconf{} days after 0.21.0,
and the two of 21~September~2026, which followed the platform pushes of June and August: the bias described above.

\subsection{What became of the exposed nodes}
\label{sec:fates}
On the day Lightning Labs disclosed its batch, \ExpLLBatchDisc\% of live LND nodes, holding \ExpCapLLBatchDisc\% of
live LND capacity, were on a line older than 0.19 (Fig.~\ref{fig:cases}). Of the \CohortLLBatch{} exposed nodes,
\CohortUpLLBatch{} had upgraded by the cutoff, \CohortLeftLLBatch{} were no longer live and \CohortStillLLBatch{}
(\PctCohortStillLLBatch\%) were still live on an affected line. Morehouse's disclosure of three LND vulnerabilities
fixed in the same release, on 4~December~2025~\cite{morehouse2025stalling,morehouse2025failback2,morehouse2025inbox},
found \ExpLndSweeperDisc\% of live LND nodes exposed; four months later \PctCohortStillLndSweeper\% of that cohort was
still live on an affected line, \PctCohortUpLndSweeper\% had upgraded and \PctCohortLeftLndSweeper\% were gone. The
exposed LND nodes are small: their share of capacity is about half their share of nodes.

CLN's exposure fell much further. Of the \CohortClnGossipd{} CLN nodes exposed to the gossipd vulnerability at
disclosure, \CohortUpClnGossipd{} (\PctCohortUpClnGossipd\%) had upgraded by the cutoff and \CohortLeftClnGossipd{}
(\PctCohortLeftClnGossipd\%) were no longer live, leaving \CohortStillClnGossipd{}; exposure fell from
\ExpClnGossipdDisc\% of live CLN nodes to \ExpClnGossipdCutoff\% (from \ExpCapClnGossipdDisc\% to
\ExpCapClnGossipdCutoff\% of capacity). Much of the fall is nodes leaving rather than upgrading, as in the CLN
shutdown we measured earlier~\cite{kumar2026clientdiversity}; leaving also ends the exposure, and restarting offline
was what the maintainers asked of operators who could not upgrade.

Release lines show who followed that call. Of the \OffCallNewestNodes{} live CLN nodes on the newest line
(\OffCallNewestLine) on 25~August, \OffCallNewestLeft\% were no longer live two days later, against
\OffCallBehindLeft\% of the \OffCallBehindNodes{} on older lines (odds ratio \OffCallOddsRatio, Fisher's exact test
$p=\OffCallP$). In the \OffCallOrdWindows{} two-day windows of August before the call, the medians were
\OffCallOrdNewestMedian\% and \OffCallOrdBehindMedian\%. The highest, \OffCallOrdNewestMax\% and
\OffCallOrdBehindMax\% from \OffCallOrdMaxDay, came during the closing waves of early
August~\cite{kumar2026clientdiversity}, and only \OffCallOrdMaxDarkShare\% of the nodes that left then still had
announced channels; at the call, \OffCallNewestDarkN{} of the \OffCallNewestLeftN{} newest-line nodes that left still had
theirs, so they had gone offline rather than lost their channels. The gap is not one of transport: among Tor-only nodes
\OffCallNewestTor\% and \OffCallBehindTor\% left, among the others \OffCallNewestOther\% and \OffCallBehindOther\%. At
most \OffCallMovedMax\% of either group changed line in those two days; the next CLN release came out on 28~August. The operators who took their nodes offline were mostly those who keep up with
releases, already patched against both July vulnerabilities; the nodes still on lines those vulnerabilities affect
mostly neither upgraded nor left. This is also why CLN's exposure briefly rose at the call (Fig.~\ref{fig:cases}).

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:robust}
Table~\ref{tab:robustness} varies the definitions behind the four case studies. A 7- or 28-day liveness window moves
the exposure by about one percentage point. Counting the fleet as nodes would lower LND's exposure, since the fleet
ran fixed lines, to \RobFleetLLBatchDisc\% instead of \ExpLLBatchDisc\% for Lightning Labs' batch. The strict lower
bounds for LND sit well below the likely-line exposure, because a newer line with some default features switched off
can produce the bits of an older one (\S\ref{sec:inference}); the likely line assumes the fewest such changes. The
exposed nodes are not stale entries in our view of the graph: \RobSignedLLBatchDisc\% of the LND nodes exposed to
Lightning Labs' batch had signed their current announcement, with the affected line's feature bits, in the 14~days
before disclosure (against \RobSignedAllLLBatchDisc\% of all live LND nodes). CLN nodes re-sign far less often
(\RobSignedAllClnGossipdDisc\% of all live CLN nodes within 14~days), so CLN exposure rests more on the absence of a
new announcement.
