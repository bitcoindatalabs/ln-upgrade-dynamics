\section{Platforms Set the Pace}
\label{sec:waves}

\subsection{Dating platform releases}
Gossip does not name the platform a node runs on, but the signing time of a node's first announcement on a new line
can be set against platform releases dated to the minute. For Umbrel we take the merge time of every pull request
that changed the version of its LND or CLN app from 2024 to the cutoff~\cite{umbrel2026apps}. A BTCPay Server
deployment updates only when its operator runs the update, which installs the LND or CLN version BTCPay pins at that
moment, so its routine version bumps reach nodes over weeks; we take its security release 2.4.2 of 7~August~2026,
which asked every operator to update at once~\cite{btcpay2026advisory}. Start9, RaspiBlitz, myNode and hosted
services publish through channels we cannot date to the day and are not covered.

\subsection{Upgrade waves}
Upgrades arrive in bursts (Fig.~\ref{fig:waves}). We call a day a wave day for a line when more nodes moved to it
than on 99\% of all (day, line) pairs, and merge consecutive wave days on one line into an event. Outside the fleet,
LND had \NWaveEvents{} events of \WaveMinNodes--\WaveMaxNodes{} nodes, \WaveMinDays--\WaveMaxDays{} days after the
line's release. They are not gossip catching up after a gap: in every event, \WaveMinSignedTwo\% of the nodes had
signed their new announcement within two days before we first saw it.

The three largest LND events, \WaveUmbrelMinNodes--\WaveUmbrelMaxNodes{} nodes each, follow Umbrel's releases
of LND 0.19.1, 0.20.0 and 0.21.1: in each, at least \WaveUmbrelSignedMin\% of the nodes signed their new
announcement within 48~hours after the merge (median \WaveUmbrelHoursMin--\WaveUmbrelHoursMax{}~hours after) and at
most \WaveUmbrelBeforeMax\% in the 24~hours before. These nodes are far more often Tor-only
(\WaveUmbrelTorMin--\WaveUmbrelTorMax\%, against \WaveUmbrelBaseMin--\WaveUmbrelBaseMax\% of all live LND nodes), small
(median live capacity \WaveUmbrelCapMin--\WaveUmbrelCapMax{}~BTC, against \WaveUmbrelOtherCapMin--\WaveUmbrelOtherCapMax{}~BTC
for the other upgraders to the same line) and established (median \WaveUmbrelAgeMin--\WaveUmbrelAgeMax{} days in our
data). Their timing is the platform's: each wave came \WaveUmbrelDaysMin--\WaveUmbrelDaysMax{} days after LND's own
release, when Umbrel shipped it. The managed fleet of \S\ref{sec:data} kept the same schedule: \FleetWaveNodes{} of its
nodes moved to LND 0.20 on \FleetWaveDay{}, \FleetWaveSigned\% of them within 48~hours after Umbrel published 0.20.0,
with the same median lag (\FleetWaveHours{}~hours) as the platform's other users.

A fourth event is different: \WaveBTCPayNodes{} nodes on \WaveBTCPayDay{}, \WaveBTCPaySigned\% of them signed within
48~hours after BTCPay's security release (median \WaveBTCPayHours{}~hours). Unlike the Umbrel waves they are mostly
clearnet (\WaveBTCPayTor\% Tor-only, against \WaveBTCPayBase\%), and \WaveBTCPayOlder{} of them came from lines older
than 0.20, skipping at least one release. They look like operators who had not moved to a newer LND line in months
and did so when the advisory for their merchant software told them to update; that advisory concerned BTCPay
itself, not LND. Of the remaining
two events, one (\WaveEarlyNodes{} nodes, \WaveEarlyDay) was signed before Umbrel merged LND \WaveEarlyVersion{}
(\WaveEarlyBefore\% in the 24~hours before), so that release cannot explain it, and one (\WaveNoneNodes{} nodes,
\WaveNoneDay) matches no release we can date. CLN shows the same pattern at its smaller scale: its one attributed
event, \ClnWaveNodes{} nodes on \ClnWaveDay{}, followed Umbrel's release of CLN 26.04.1 (\ClnWaveSigned\% within
48~hours, median \ClnWaveHours{}~hours).

\subsection{Beyond the waves}
Platform releases account for a share of upgrades out of proportion to the time they cover. Since June~2025,
\PlatShareLND\% of LND upgrades whose day is known to within two days were signed within 48~hours after a platform
release that ships the new line, although these windows cover \PlatTimeLND\% of the time; for CLN, \PlatShareCLN\%
against \PlatTimeCLN\%. Since the platforms we cannot date are left out, these shares are lower bounds. The pushes
also check the inference: the nodes that changed line right after a platform shipped a new LND line landed on the
line it shipped (\S\ref{sec:validation}).
