Every Lightning implementation disclosed security vulnerabilities in 2026, and every advisory told operators to
upgrade. Whether they do has never been measured, because Lightning gossip carries no software version. We show that
the release line of a public node can be read from the feature bits it announces: matched against the source of
every release of LND, Core Lightning, Eclair and LDK, the most likely line is exact for \NVerNodeLinesExact{} of
\NVerNodeLines{} node--line pairs of Core Lightning nodes that state their version. Applied to \DataUsableDays{} daily snapshots of
the public graph from 2023 to 2026, it shows that the network patches on release and platform schedules, not on
disclosures. Half of the LND nodes on an older line upgrade within \MedianLNDAll{} days of a release, while small
nodes and nodes that skipped earlier releases lag far behind. The largest movements are node platforms shipping a
release to all their users: the three largest upgrade waves came about a day after the Umbrel app store released a
new LND version, and since June~2025, \PlatShareLND\% of LND upgrades fell in the \PlatTimeLND\% of the time just
after a platform release. Disclosure, a median \FixToDiscMedian{} days after the fix, reached only operators already
close to current: of \NResp{} disclosures we can test, only Core Lightning's of July~2026 raised the rate at which
nodes left affected releases, \RROpClnRecent-fold among nodes one release line behind, while nodes further behind
moved when BTCPay Server's own advisory had their operators update. Core Lightning's call to restart offline was
likewise followed twice as often by nodes already on the newest line as by nodes behind. No LND disclosure produced a
detectable increase;
two months after Lightning Labs' August~2026 batch, \PctCohortStillLLBatch\% of the nodes exposed at disclosure still
ran an affected release. The lever on that tail is the platforms that package releases.
