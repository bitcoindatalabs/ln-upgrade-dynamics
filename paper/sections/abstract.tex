Every implementation of Bitcoin's Lightning payment network disclosed security vulnerabilities in 2026, and every
advisory told operators to upgrade. Whether they do has never been measured, because Lightning gossip carries no software version. Matching the
feature bits a node announces against the source of every LND, Core Lightning, Eclair and LDK release, we show that
gossip reveals a node's release line after all. Where Core Lightning nodes state their version, the inferred line is
exact for \NVerNodeLinesExact{} of \NVerNodeLines{} node--line pairs. Across \DataUsableDays{} daily snapshots of the
public graph from 2023 to 2026, the network patches on release and platform schedules, not on disclosures. Half of
LND nodes upgrade within \MedianLNDAll{} days of a release; small nodes and those that skipped releases lag far
behind. The three largest upgrade waves came about a day after the Umbrel app store shipped a new LND version. Since
June~2025, \PlatShareLND\% of LND upgrades came in the 48~hours after a platform release, windows that cover
\PlatTimeLND\% of the time. Disclosures, a median \FixToDiscMedian{} days after the fix, moved few operators: of
\NResp{} we can test, only Core Lightning's of July~2026 sped up upgrades, and only among nodes one release line
behind. Its later call to restart offline was followed mostly by nodes already patched. Two months after Lightning
Labs' August~2026 batch, \PctCohortStillLLBatch\% of the LND nodes exposed at disclosure still ran an affected
release. For maintainers, the lever on the nodes that lag is the platforms that package their releases.
