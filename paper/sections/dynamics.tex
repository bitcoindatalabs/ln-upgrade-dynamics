\section{How Lightning Nodes Upgrade}
\label{sec:dynamics}

\subsection{One operator, many nodes}
Before measuring upgrades, one population has to be set apart. From \FleetFirstDay{}, hundreds of LND nodes appear
with aliases of the form \texttt{LNT.<name>}, one or two channels each, most of them to a handful of hubs with the
same prefix, and identical Lightning Terminal feature sets. At its peak on \FleetPeakDay{} the fleet ran
\FleetPeakNodes{} nodes, \FleetPeakShare\% of all live LND nodes, with \FleetPeakCap{}~BTC of live capacity against
\FleetPeakTotalCap{}~BTC for live LND as a whole; at the cutoff it still ran \FleetCutoffNodes{} nodes
(\FleetCutoffShare\% of live LND nodes). The fleet upgrades as one: \FleetWaveNodes{} of its nodes moved to LND 0.20
on \FleetWaveDay{}. Its upgrades are one operator's decisions, so we exclude its nodes (by alias prefix, or by having
at least half their channels to a fleet hub) from every per-node statistic below and describe them here instead. The
fleet also illustrates why node counts are a poor measure on the Lightning Network~\cite{kumar2026clientdiversity}:
for half a year, more than a fifth of the live LND nodes we observe belonged to one operator and held less than a
thousandth of LND's live capacity.

\subsection{Adoption of new release lines}
Fig.~\ref{fig:linemix} shows the release-line mix of live LND and CLN nodes. Each new LND line displaces its
predecessor within a few months but leaves a tail: at the cutoff, \PctOldLndLive\% of live LND nodes were still on a
line older than 0.19, released in May~2025. New lines reached half of live LND nodes \TFiftyLndMin--\TFiftyLndMax{}
days after release (Fig.~\ref{fig:adoption}); half of live capacity moved as fast or faster for 0.18 and 0.19
(\TFiftyCapLndEighteen{} and \TFiftyCapLndNineteen{} days) but more slowly for 0.20 (\TFiftyCapLndTwenty{} days).
Counting the fleet as independent nodes would put LND 0.20 at 50\% of nodes after \TFiftyLndTwentyWithFleet{} days
instead of \TFiftyLndTwenty{}. CLN lines took \TFiftyClnMin--\TFiftyClnMax{} days. Every LND line from 0.18 on was
announced by a few nodes \FirstSeenLndMin--\FirstSeenLndMax{} days before its release: developers and early adopters
running unreleased builds on mainnet.

\subsection{Time to upgrade}
Adoption curves mix upgrades with nodes arriving and leaving. To follow operators, we take every node that is live
on an older line on the release day of LND 0.19, 0.20 or 0.21 or of a CLN line from 23.11 on, and measure the time
until it is first seen on the new line or a later one. Snapshot gaps leave the upgrade day inside an interval (for
\PctIntervalNarrow\% of LND upgrades it is at most two days wide); nodes that stop being live before upgrading are
censored at their last live day. The share upgraded by day $t$ is the nonparametric maximum-likelihood estimate for
interval-censored data~\cite{turnbull1976empirical}. Half of the LND nodes upgraded within \MedianLNDAll{} days and
\ByYearLNDAll\% within a year; for CLN the median is \MedianCLNAll{} days. Size matters at the bottom: the smallest
quarter of LND nodes by live capacity took a median \MedianLNDSmall{} days, the largest quarter \MedianLNDLarge{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ttu}).

Table~\ref{tab:hazard} fits a discrete-time model of the weekly probability of upgrading for LND. Larger nodes
upgrade sooner (odds ratio \ORCapacity{} per tenfold capacity, \ORChannels{} per tenfold channel count), and so do
Tor-only nodes (\ORTor{}). A node more than one line behind
when the release ships is far less likely to move (\ORBehind{}): the tail is made of nodes that skipped releases
before. Nodes with LND's default colour are no slower (\ORDefaultColor{}), and nodes seen before 2024 upgrade at the
same rate as newer ones (\OROld{}).

\subsection{Upgrade waves}
Upgrades arrive in bursts (Fig.~\ref{fig:waves}). Outside the fleet, on \NWaves{} days at least
\WaveMinNodes{} LND nodes, and up to \WaveMaxNodes{}, moved to a new line, \WaveMinDays--\WaveMaxDays{} days after its
release. These are not new or empty nodes: their median live capacity (\WaveMedianCap{}~BTC, against
\WaveOtherCap{}~BTC for other upgraders of the same line) and their median age in our data (\WaveMedianAge{} days)
rule out a fleet of fresh nodes. \NWavesTor{} of the waves are more often Tor-only than the network
(\WaveTorMin--\WaveTorMax\% of the wave, against \TorBaseMin--\TorBaseMax\% of all live LND nodes), and about half of
each shares one feature set: the pattern of node platforms that ship an update to all their users at once, though
gossip cannot name the platform. The remaining wave is different: \ClearWaveNodes{} mostly clearnet nodes
(\ClearWaveTor\% Tor-only) moved to LND \ClearWaveLine{} on \ClearWaveDay, the day after BTCPay Server published its
security release 2.4.2, which bundles LND 0.21.1~\cite{btcpay2026advisory}.
