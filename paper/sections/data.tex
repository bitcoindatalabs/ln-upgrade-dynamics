\section{Data and Vantage Point}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Gossip snapshots}
We take a daily dump of the public graph (\texttt{describegraph}) from our own LND node and keep, per day, every
node announcement (feature bits, alias, colour, addresses, last-update time) and every channel direction (the policy
a node sets for forwarding towards its peer, with capacity, the \emph{disabled} flag and last-update time). An
announcement's last-update time is the moment its node signed it, so the first announcement that shows a new release's
bits dates an upgrade more finely than the daily snapshot does (\S\ref{sec:waves}). The snapshots run from May~2023
to the data cutoff of 13~October~2026. A per-day quality check discards dumps that
are partial (fewer than half the channels of their period), stale (fewer than half the channels updated within two
weeks) or error responses written while the node's wallet was locked; \DataUsableDays{} days remain. Daily coverage is
dense from November~2023, with gaps in May--June~2024 (partial dumps), August~2024 and January--May~2025 (wallet
locked after restarts). Upgrade times observed across a gap are known only to lie inside it; we treat them as
interval-censored (\S\ref{sec:dynamics}).

\paragraph{Vantage}
All snapshots are one node's view. LND prunes channels that have not been updated for about two weeks, and our
view widened in July~2024 (from about 8.4k to 11--13k nodes with channels), after the node's own peer set grew. We
compare levels within a period and report shares, which are less sensitive to the view; in particular, the few
live nodes on releases from before 2021 appear only from July~2024. Only announced (public) nodes are visible:
mobile wallets and services behind unannounced channels, where much of LDK and Eclair runs, are not.

\paragraph{Who is still there}
Nodes that stop running do not leave the graph at once: their peers mark the channels towards them disabled, and
the channels stay announced until they are closed or pruned. An offline node cannot announce anything, so liveness
must be read from its peers' side~\cite{kumar2026clientdiversity}. We call a node \emph{live} on a day when at least
one channel direction towards it, set by a peer, is enabled and was updated in the previous 14~days; its \emph{live
capacity} is the capacity of those channels. Results with 7- and 28-day windows are in
Table~\ref{tab:robustness}. The distinction matters. On the cutoff day, \NOldLndChan{} LND nodes with channels
(outside the managed fleet described next) announced a release older than 0.19, but only \NOldLndLive{} of them were
live; counting every announced node would put the share of LND nodes on those releases at \PctOldLndNaive\%, against
\PctOldLndLive\% of live nodes.

\paragraph{A managed fleet}
Our previous paper found a managed fleet of small LND nodes with aliases of the form \texttt{LNT.<name>}, one or two
channels each, mostly to a handful of hubs with the same prefix, and counted up to 1,980 announced fleet
nodes~\cite{kumar2026clientdiversity}. We use its definition (the alias prefix, or at least half of a node's channels
to a fleet hub) but count live nodes. The fleet appeared on \FleetFirstDay{} and peaked on \FleetPeakDay{} at
\FleetPeakNodes{} live nodes, \FleetPeakShare\% of all live LND nodes, with \FleetPeakCap{}~BTC of live capacity
against \FleetPeakTotalCap{}~BTC for live LND as a whole; at the cutoff \FleetCutoffNodes{} of its nodes were still
live (\FleetCutoffShare\% of live LND nodes). Its nodes share one Lightning Terminal feature set and upgrade in step
(\S\ref{sec:waves}), so they are not independent operators, and we exclude them from every per-node statistic.
